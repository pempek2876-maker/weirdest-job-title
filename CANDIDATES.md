Full round-robin (91 pairings): candidate 1 wins all 13 of its pairings,

Revision notes: candidate 2 was first scored strange 5, then re-scored 4 —
"dogs ate homework" is a worn classroom cliché, so the twist is a notch less
fresh than billionaires' group chats. Candidate 3 lost a point on funny and
instant: its two-step twist (cats, on behalf of dogs) takes a beat longer to
land than a single twist. Candidate 12 lost a point on funny: "Corporate
Division" is a second, weaker twist that dilutes the dog-excuse joke.
Candidates 13 and 14 sit lowest on strange/funny: an ice-cube inspector and
a password rememberer are odd chores, but neither twist is gloriously odd
enough to beat a job that names billionaires.

Why the winner fits the brief: it is unmistakably a weird job title, not a
generic oddity (criterion 1); the twist — billionaires' group chats — is a
gloriously specific oddity nobody has heard of as a workplace (2); the laugh
lands on the first read, picturing someone typing apologies into a billionaire
group chat (3); it reads exactly like a real corporate title, "Senior X for
Y" (4); and at 47 characters it is taken in at a glance (5).
